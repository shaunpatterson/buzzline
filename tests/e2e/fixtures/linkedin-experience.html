About

I have spent the last decade selling infrastructure software to financial services companies, first at IBM, then at Twilio and Slack, and now at Baseten. Most of that time has gone into learning how large banks actually buy technology. Decisions are slow, risk teams have the final word, and the people who will use a product are rarely the ones who sign for it. The deals that worked were the ones where we spent months understanding the operating constraints before we talked about features. I write here about what I have learned from those cycles, what I got wrong, and how AI inference is changing the conversation with engineering leaders who now own budgets that used to sit with procurement.
