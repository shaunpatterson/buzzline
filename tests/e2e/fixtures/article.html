The committee's long evening

The committee met on three occasions to discuss the proposal and its consequences for the region. Members disagreed about the cost. A vote was taken late in the evening. The chair asked for a second reading before any decision was recorded in the minutes of the meeting.

Several members argued that the proposal would change how the region plans its roads for a decade. Others said the cost estimates were optimistic. The finance officer was asked to return with revised figures and a schedule for repayment over the following years.

The public gallery was full for most of the session. Residents from the northern villages spoke about traffic through their streets. A farmer described how the current junction floods each winter. The chair thanked them and promised that their comments would be considered.

After a short break the engineers presented three options for the route. The first followed the river and was the cheapest to build. The second crossed the hills and avoided the villages entirely. The third combined parts of both and was the slowest to complete.

Debate on the options lasted well into the evening. Members asked about the effect on wildlife along the river. They asked how long each option would close existing roads. The engineers answered most questions but promised written replies on the rest within a week.

When the vote finally came, the second option won by a narrow margin. Two members abstained and asked for their reasons to be noted. The chair reminded everyone that the decision could still be reviewed if the revised cost figures turned out to be much higher.
